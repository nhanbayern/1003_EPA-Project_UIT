\begin{abstract}
Accurate volatility forecasting is crucial for financial risk management, particularly in emerging markets characterized by heavy-tailed return distributions and pronounced volatility clustering. This paper presents a comprehensive comparative framework evaluating traditional GARCH models against a novel hybrid LSTM-GARCH approach for volatility forecasting and Value-at-Risk estimation in the Vietnamese stock market. Our methodology integrates the econometric foundations of GARCH(1,1) and GJR-GARCH models with Long Short-Term Memory networks through a multiplicative correction mechanism that incorporates Heterogeneous Autoregressive (HAR) realized volatility features. Using market-capitalization weighted returns and major equity indices from 2020 onwards, we implement Student-t distributional assumptions with estimated degrees of freedom ranging from 2.5 to 2.8 to accommodate the heavy-tailed behavior observed across all datasets. Empirical results reveal nuanced performance characteristics: while traditional GARCH models achieve superior mean squared error performance, the hybrid LSTM-GARCH model demonstrates competitive quasi-likelihood scores and significantly improved Value-at-Risk calibration. Kupiec proportion of failures tests confirm that the hybrid approach generates well-calibrated 95\% VaR forecasts that consistently pass backtesting procedures, whereas traditional models occasionally exhibit violation rates deviating from nominal levels. Diebold-Mariano forecast comparison tests indicate that model superiority depends critically on the chosen loss function, with the hybrid approach showing particular strength under quasi-likelihood and absolute error criteria. These findings suggest that the proposed framework offers practical advantages for risk management applications requiring robust probability forecasts rather than point estimate accuracy, providing a valuable tool for financial institutions operating in emerging market environments.

\section{Introduction}

Accurate volatility forecasting is essential for financial risk management, particularly in emerging markets where traditional econometric models may inadequately capture complex market dynamics. Vietnamese financial markets exhibit pronounced volatility clustering, leverage effects, and heavy-tailed return distributions that challenge conventional modeling assumptions.

This study develops and evaluates a hybrid LSTM-GARCH framework for volatility forecasting in Vietnamese equity markets. By integrating the theoretical foundations of GARCH modeling with Long Short-Term Memory networks through multiplicative corrections, we address limitations of traditional econometric approaches while preserving interpretability for practical risk management applications.

Our methodology compares GARCH(1,1), GJR-GARCH, and the proposed LSTM-GARCH hybrid using Vietnamese market data from 2020 onwards, employing Student-t distributional assumptions and comprehensive backtesting procedures to evaluate both statistical accuracy and practical risk management performance.

\subsection{Main Contributions}

This research provides three key contributions to financial risk modeling. First, we develop a multiplicative LSTM-GARCH hybrid incorporating Heterogeneous Autoregressive (HAR) volatility features specifically designed for emerging market characteristics. The architecture preserves GARCH theoretical properties while enabling adaptive learning through neural network corrections.

Second, we implement a distributional similarity-based data splitting methodology using Kolmogorov-Smirnov optimization, ensuring consistent statistical properties across training, validation, and test sets for robust model evaluation.

Third, we provide comprehensive empirical evidence demonstrating that while traditional GARCH models achieve superior point forecast accuracy, the hybrid approach offers significant advantages in quasi-likelihood performance and Value-at-Risk calibration, with consistent success in Kupiec backtesting procedures across multiple Vietnamese datasets.
\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Econometric Volatility Models}

Volatility forecasting is a fundamental topic in financial econometrics due to its importance in risk management and derivative pricing. Bollerslev (1986) introduced the Generalized Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity (GARCH) model \cite{ref1}, which captures volatility clustering and has become the standard framework for modeling financial volatility. Hansen and Lunde (2005) later showed that the simple GARCH(1,1) model remains a strong benchmark in volatility forecasting \cite{ref2}. 

Several extensions have been proposed to capture additional characteristics of financial markets. Glosten et al. (1993) introduced the GJR-GARCH model to model asymmetric volatility responses \cite{ref3}, while Baillie et al. (1996) proposed the FIGARCH model to capture long-memory effects in volatility dynamics \cite{ref4}. Although these econometric models are theoretically interpretable and widely used in practice, they are limited in capturing complex nonlinear patterns in financial time series.

\subsection{Machine Learning for Volatility Forecasting}

To address the nonlinear nature of financial markets, machine learning models have increasingly been applied to volatility forecasting. Neural network architectures such as multilayer perceptrons, recurrent neural networks, and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks have demonstrated strong ability to capture nonlinear temporal dependencies.

Kim and Won (2018) proposed a hybrid LSTM-GARCH model for stock market volatility forecasting and showed that the hybrid framework improves prediction accuracy compared with traditional econometric models \cite{ref5}. However, many machine learning approaches rely heavily on data-driven learning and often lack financial interpretability or the ability to incorporate volatility stylized facts identified in econometric studies.

\subsection{Hybrid Econometric–Deep Learning Models}

To combine the strengths of econometric and machine learning approaches, recent studies have explored hybrid volatility forecasting frameworks. Kakade et al. (2022) proposed a GARCH-LSTM ensemble model for Value-at-Risk forecasting and demonstrated improved risk prediction performance \cite{ref6}. More recently, Zhao et al. (2024) introduced a GARCH-NN framework that integrates the structural properties of GARCH models into neural network architectures \cite{ref7}. 

Although these hybrid approaches improve prediction accuracy, challenges remain in balancing model interpretability and nonlinear learning capability. Motivated by these limitations, this study proposes a hybrid volatility forecasting framework that integrates econometric volatility modeling with deep learning to better capture complex financial market dynamics.

\section{Proposed Solutions and Techniques}

\subsection{Conditional Volatility Modeling Framework}

Our methodology employs a comprehensive framework comparing traditional econometric models with hybrid neural network approaches for volatility forecasting. The conditional volatility modeling assumes returns follow the decomposition $r_t = \sigma_t \varepsilon_t$, where $\sigma_t^2$ represents time-varying conditional variance and $\varepsilon_t$ denotes standardized innovations.

\subsection{Benchmark GARCH Models}

We implement two baseline specifications serving as econometric benchmarks. The standard GARCH(1,1) model captures volatility clustering through:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_t^2 = \omega + \alpha r_{t-1}^2 + \beta \sigma_{t-1}^2
\end{equation}
where $\omega > 0$, $\alpha \geq 0$, $\beta \geq 0$, and $\alpha + \beta < 1$ ensure non-negativity and covariance stationarity.

The GJR-GARCH extension incorporates leverage effects via an asymmetric component:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_t^2 = \omega + \alpha r_{t-1}^2 + \beta \sigma_{t-1}^2 + \gamma r_{t-1}^2 \mathbf{I}(r_{t-1} < 0)
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{I}(\cdot)$ denotes the indicator function and $\gamma \geq 0$ captures additional volatility from negative shocks.

\subsection{Hybrid LSTM-GARCH Architecture}

The proposed hybrid model integrates LSTM neural networks with GARCH foundations through a multiplicative correction mechanism consisting of three components:

\subsubsection{Enhanced GARCH Base with HAR Features}
The base variance specification extends traditional GARCH with Heterogeneous Autoregressive (HAR) realized volatility components  \cite{ref9}:
\begin{equation}
\text{\sigma}_t = \omega + \alpha r_{t-1}^2 + \beta \sigma_{t-1}^2 + \lambda r_{t-1}^2 \mathbf{I}(r_{t-1} < 0) + \phi_1 RV_{1,t-1} + \phi_5 RV_{5,t-1} + \phi_{20} RV_{20,t-1}
\end{equation}
where $RV_{k,t}$ represents $k$-period realized volatility measures computed as moving averages of squared returns.

\subsubsection{LSTM Correction Mechanism}
The LSTM component processes standardized shock $s_t = r_{t-1}/\sqrt{\sigma_{t-1}^2 + \epsilon}$ and log variance $v_t = \log(\text{base\_var}_t + \epsilon)$ through element-wise gate operations:
\begin{align}
f_t &= \sigma(W_f \odot s_t + U_f \odot v_t + b_f) \\
i_t &= \sigma(W_i \odot s_t + U_i \odot v_t + b_i) \\
\tilde{C}_t &= \tanh(W_c \odot s_t + U_c \odot v_t + b_c)
\end{align}
where $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication. Cell state evolution follows $C_t = f_t \odot C_{t-1} + i_t \odot \tilde{C}_t$.

\subsubsection{Multiplicative Integration}
The final volatility forecast combines GARCH dynamics with adaptive LSTM corrections:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_t^2 = \text{base\_var}_t \times (1 + 0.05 \times \tanh(C_t^T v))
\end{equation}
This multiplicative structure preserves positive definiteness while enabling learned pattern adjustments.

\subsection{Student-t Distribution Framework}

All models assume standardized innovations follow Student-t distributions to accommodate heavy-tailed behavior:
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_t | \mathcal{F}_{t-1} \sim t_{\nu}(0, 1)
\end{equation}
where $\nu > 4$ ensures finite fourth moments and is estimated from empirical distribution fitting.

\subsection{Value-at-Risk Estimation}

VaR forecasts utilize Student-t quantiles with variance scaling:
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\text{VaR}}_{\alpha,t} = t_{\nu}^{-1}(\alpha) \cdot \hat{\sigma}_t \cdot \sqrt{\frac{\nu - 2}{\nu}}
\end{equation}
where $t_{\nu}^{-1}(\alpha)$ represents the $\alpha$-quantile of the Student-t distribution and the scaling factor ensures proper variance normalization under heavy-tailed assumptions.

The methodology employs a composite loss function combining quasi-likelihood estimation with spike penalty regularization:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}[\log(\sigma_t^2) + \frac{r_t^2}{\sigma_t^2}] + \lambda \mathbb{E}[\text{ReLU}(r_t^2 - \sigma_t^2)^2]
\end{equation}
ensuring robust parameter estimation while preventing excessive volatility under-prediction.
\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

The experimental evaluation employs a rigorous out-of-sample forecasting protocol to assess volatility prediction performance across three Vietnamese financial market datasets. The evaluation framework uses KS-optimal data splitting, which determines training, validation, and test partitions by maximizing distributional similarity using Kolmogorov-Smirnov test statistics. This approach yields training sets of 946 observations, validation sets of 184 observations, and remaining observations allocated to test sets.

The three datasets span the period from 2020 onwards and include: (1) market-capitalization weighted stock returns constructed from VN30 constituent stocks, (2) VN30 Index returns, and (3) VN Index returns. All return series exhibit heavy-tailed behavior and are well-fitted by Student-t distributions with degrees of freedom parameters $\nu = 2.5165$ (stock returns), $\nu = 2.7537$ (VN30 Index), and $\nu = 2.7542$ (VN Index), respectively.

The forecasting framework employs 60-day sequence lengths for the LSTM-GARCH model, with benchmark comparisons against GARCH(1,1) and GJR-GARCH models. Traditional models use rolling one-step-ahead forecasts with Student-t distributional assumptions to ensure time-aligned evaluation periods.

Performance evaluation encompasses multiple metrics: Mean Squared Error (MSE) for volatility accuracy, Quasi-Maximum Likelihood (QLIKE) for likelihood-based performance, violation rates for Value-at-Risk (VaR) calibration, Kupiec likelihood ratio tests for VaR statistical adequacy, and Diebold-Mariano tests for statistical significance of performance differences. VaR evaluation employs 95\% confidence levels computed from predicted volatility using Student-t quantiles with dataset-specific degrees of freedom.

The experimental results demonstrate several key empirical findings consistent across all three datasets. Traditional GARCH models consistently achieve superior MSE performance, with GJR-GARCH typically delivering the lowest mean squared errors for realized volatility forecasting. However, the LSTM-GARCH hybrid model systematically outperforms both GARCH variants on QLIKE scores across all datasets, suggesting superior likelihood-based distributional modeling capabilities.

Most critically for risk management applications, the LSTM-GARCH model provides substantially improved VaR calibration. Violation rates remain very close to the theoretical 5\% threshold across all datasets (4.55\%, 4.56\%, and 4.87\% for market-cap weighted returns, VN30 Index, and VN Index, respectively). In contrast, traditional GARCH models exhibit systematic VaR underperformance with excess violations ranging from 8.14\% to 11.04\%, indicating significant underestimation of tail risks.

The Kupiec test results strongly support the superior risk modeling performance of LSTM-GARCH, with consistently high p-values (0.7103, 0.7199, and 0.9164) indicating excellent VaR statistical adequacy. Traditional GARCH models frequently fail the Kupiec adequacy test, with p-values well below the 0.05 significance threshold across multiple datasets.

While Diebold-Mariano tests for QLIKE performance show mixed statistical significance, the consistent direction of improvement favoring LSTM-GARCH (negative test statistics) across all datasets suggests meaningful practical advantages. The strongest evidence appears for the market-cap weighted returns, where LSTM-GARCH improvements approach statistical significance.

These results highlight the practical value of the hybrid LSTM-GARCH approach for financial risk management, particularly in emerging markets where accurate tail risk assessment is crucial for portfolio management and regulatory compliance.
